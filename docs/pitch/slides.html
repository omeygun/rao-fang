The problem

Foreign guests leave the farm.
What they thought leaves with them.

Guests write in English, 中文, 한국어 Hosts read Thai — and think in Kham Mueang Signal on the farm is weak or gone

So the host never learns which parts of the tour guests valued, what went wrong, or what they wanted to buy.

Meet Noor

A coffee farmer in the hills of Chiang Rai who hosts foreign visitors on her farm.

6–7 a month

foreign visitors walk the farm, pick cherries, roast and taste

Thai + Kham Mueang

Thai is her written language; Northern Thai is how she thinks and talks

One Android phone

no laptop, and patchy signal where the tour happens

She wants to know which parts of the tour guests value — and what they would buy.

The product

One loop, all on the phone.

1

The guest answers

At the end of the tour, in their own language: 👍 / 👎 per tour step, then speak or type. Works offline.

2

On-device AI sorts it

Into 13 tour topics, liked or disliked — and flags anything it isn't sure about.

3

The host reviews in Thai

Weekly cards she can read or hear aloud, with the real guest comments behind every number.

The AI suggests. The host decides.

Live app · the guest

The guest answers in their own language.

  • Picks 한국어 (Korean)
  • Agrees to a plain-language consent
  • Rates each tour step 👍 / 👎
  • Types what to improve — and what they'd buy
  • Hands the phone back. Nothing leaves it.
Live app · the host

The host gets insights she can check.

  • % positive, top strength, top problem
  • One card per tour topic with 👍 / 👎 counts
  • 🔊 reads each card aloud in Thai
  • Every number opens the real guest comments
  • Unclear comments wait for a person

English captions are on for this demo — the host sees Thai only.

Local language

It speaks the host's language — even the local one.

  • Insights are fixed Thai sentences filled with counts and real quotes — no AI-written text to go wrong
  • The host teaches it Kham Mueang words; her own notes are converted to standard Thai
  • 38 starter words to confirm or correct — because dialects vary village to village
